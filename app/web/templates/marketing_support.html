Frequently asked

How does ArborDocs decide what counts as a "decision"?

An LLM pipeline reads new messages in your watched channels and extracts statements that read as durable technical or process decisions — "let's go with Postgres," "all API responses must be paginated." Every candidate goes to a human review queue before it becomes a record; nothing is published automatically.

Does the bot read everything in our Discord?

Only the channels you explicitly select, and only messages posted after you connect. It never reads DMs and it never posts to your server. You can remove a channel at any time.

What does "reconciliation" actually check?

For each decision, ArborDocs surfaces the related code and docs in your connected repo by embedding similarity, so a reviewer can eyeball whether the codebase matches. Today it flags results as unverified — a human judges consistency. Automatic consistency/contradiction detection is on the roadmap.

What happens when a decision changes?

Decisions are a record, not a wiki. To change one, a new decision is proposed in chat and approved; it's then linked to the one it replaces (a single supersession hop), and the old decision is marked superseded. The full timeline lives in each decision's audit log.

Who can see the decision portal?

Anyone on your verified team can browse the read-only portal to answer "why did we do it this way?" Only admins can connect integrations and approve, edit, or reject decisions.

How do I get access after signing in?

Signing in with GitHub creates your account, then an admin on your team verifies it. Until then you'll see the pending-approval screen. You'll be notified the moment you're approved.
